TEAM / CJR-X Research — Folder Guide

What started this
Two essays on californiarainfall.netlify.app: "Why Does LA Have Only One TEAM Hospital?" and a companion California TEAM white paper. The core observation — Los Angeles, the country's largest Medicare market, has exactly one hospital (Cedars-Sinai) in CMS's mandatory TEAM bundled-payment program, and it's there voluntarily — led to everything else in this folder.

Folder contents

articles/
Four fully drafted, sourced essays in the same voice as the original site:
- cjr-history-team-selection.md — Los Angeles stayed a mandatory CJR market for nine years because it was expensive; San Francisco got downgraded to voluntary in 2018. Under TEAM's separate lottery, LA drew exactly one hospital (voluntarily); San Francisco drew 37 (mandatorily) — the most of any market in California. Zooms out to all 67 original CJR markets: the ones CMS once forced into mandatory participation landed on TEAM's roster at nearly double the rate of the ones it let go voluntary (35.3% vs. 21.2%), the opposite of a simple "same markets lose twice" story.
- team-lottery-international-comparison.md — CMS's stratified-random-sampling "lottery" approach isn't new (it did the same thing for CJR in 2016) and isn't normal internationally (England, Germany, China all mandated bundled/DRG payment nationwide at once, no lottery). Explains why: Section 1115A requires CMS to test models with a control group before scaling nationally.
- team-decarbonization-resilience-initiative.md — TEAM contains a small, entirely voluntary climate-reporting program with zero financial consequence. Sourced to CMS's own December 2024 webcast transcript, including the detail that CMS's own presenters used two different names for it in the same call.
- cmmi-directors-rubicon-founders.md — The last three CMMI directors under Republican administrations (Adam Boehler, Brad Smith, Abe Sutton) all came from value-based-risk companies they founded, and two of them are linked through the same firm, Rubicon Founders. Carefully hedged: documents the career pattern, explicitly does not allege financial impropriety, and names the one document (Sutton's OGE-278 financial disclosure) that would actually confirm or rule out an ongoing financial interest.

data/
- ca-team-participant-directory.csv — All 107 California hospitals on CMS's TEAM participant list: hospital name, CCN, CBSA, mandatory/voluntary status, participation dates.
- ca-team-system-affiliations.csv — Rainfall's own hospital-to-system mapping, as published, showing 48 hospitals tagged "Independent / other."
- ca-team-err-by-condition.csv — California vs. national Excess Readmission Ratio averages by condition (hip/knee, CABG, AMI, heart failure, pneumonia, COPD).
- ownership-concentration-findings.md — The audit: at least 11 of the 48 "independent" hospitals actually belong to four national for-profit chains (HCA, Prime Healthcare, UHS, KPC Health) that the affiliations file's name-matching missed. Real concentration is closer to 69/107 (64%) than the reported 58/107 (54%). Also documents smaller findings: duplicate hospital names, multi-campus systems counted as separate "hospitals."
- national-roster-findings.md — Three national-level pulls: the other 9 voluntary TEAM joiners nationwide, Kaiser's CA TEAM share vs. its real CA acute-care hospital footprint (32 of 276, not just the roster's 17 of 107), and a reconstructed CJR-mandatory-MSA-to-TEAM-selection overlap. Supporting CSVs: national-team-voluntary-joiners.csv, kaiser-ca-hospital-footprint.csv, cjr-67-msas-reconstructed-mandatory-status.csv, cjr-team-overlap.csv.

reference/raw-pulls/ — vendored source files behind national-roster-findings.md (CMS's national hospital registry, the pre-2018 CJR hospital list, the 2015-rule MSA payment-criteria file, the July 2024 CJR hospital list), kept locally so the analysis doesn't depend on CMS's URLs staying live.

facts/
- fun-facts-round-1.md — First 20 verified details, mixing international payment systems, TEAM/CJR-X mechanics, and a few odd ones (the Decarbonization Initiative's name confusion, TEAM's hurricane clause, the anti-kickback carve-out).
- fun-facts-round-2.md — Second round, from a deeper research pass: CJR's actual dollar savings and one loss year, CMMI's 4-out-of-70 model expansion record, the WISeR AI prior-authorization model launching the same month as TEAM, and the five-hospital 2009 pilot that started the whole bundled-payment concept.
