Fun facts, round 2 — deeper research pass: history, CMMI track record, AI, physician-owned hospitals

1. The entire idea of Medicare bundled payment started with five hospitals. Before CJR's 733 or TEAM's 720-plus, the 2009 Acute Care Episode (ACE) Demonstration tested it on exactly five: in San Antonio, Oklahoma City, Tulsa, Denver, and Albuquerque. CMS capped it at one hospital per market on purpose, to avoid distorting local competition.

2. CMS has only ever certified 4 models out of 70-plus for permanent nationwide expansion. Since CMMI's creation in 2011: Pioneer ACO, the Medicare Diabetes Prevention Program, ambulance-transport prior authorization (RSNAT), and Home Health Value-Based Purchasing. CJR isn't one of them, despite generating real, measured savings.

3. CMMI has increased federal spending on net, not decreased it. CBO estimates it raised Medicare spending by about $5.4 billion from 2011 to 2020, the opposite of the office's founding statutory purpose.

4. CJR actually lost Medicare money in one specific year. Performance Year 5, 2020: COVID relief flexibilities tripled the size of hospital payouts by removing downside risk almost entirely, wiping out years of accumulated savings.

5. Hospital repayments under CJR jumped over 5x in a single year, from an average $78 per episode in Performance Year 6 to $404 per episode in Year 7, once pandemic relief expired and full accountability resumed.

6. CJR's last two years outperformed its previous six combined. $112.7 million in savings across Performance Years 6-7 alone, versus $30.8 million cumulative through Year 6.

7. TEAM has a direct AI sibling that launched the exact same month. WISeR, a separate CMS Innovation Center model using AI and machine learning for Medicare prior authorization, also went live January 1, 2026, in six states: Arizona, Ohio, Oklahoma, Texas, New Jersey, Washington.

8. WISeR is a genuine first: prior authorization inside Original Medicare. Traditional fee-for-service Medicare has essentially never required it before; Medicare Advantage plans do, Original Medicare didn't. WISeR changes that for a defined list of services.

9. WISeR's vendors get paid based on how much they save Medicare. A contingency-style incentive structure, inviting direct comparison to the Medicare Advantage denial incentives that draw the most criticism, now imported into the "traditional" side of the program.

10. WISeR targets a CMS-stated number, not an outside estimate. CMS itself put the low-value spending in scope at $1.9-5.8 billion during a public webinar with the American Medical Association.

11. Japan pays hospitals per day, not per case. Its DPC/PDPS system, running since 2003, is a per-diem lump-sum tied to diagnosis groups, a genuinely different incentive from the US per-episode model: the pressure is to shorten a stay, not shrink a whole bundle.

12. Japan's system is opt-in for most hospitals. All 82 university hospitals were mandated in; ordinary community hospitals could choose. As of 2024, only about 1,786 of Japan's roughly 8,000 hospitals had opted in.

13. France phased its DRG-style system in over four years, not overnight. T2A rolled out gradually from 2004 to 2008, easing the transition between the public and private hospital sectors, a slower approach than Germany's one-year flat conversion.

14. France applies the same payment framework to public and private hospitals alike. Most countries' hospital-payment reforms are built around the public system and treat private hospitals separately; France's T2A explicitly doesn't.

15. The original CJR model included ankle replacement. Its outpatient version covered total ankle replacement alongside hip and knee, a detail nearly every retrospective summary drops.

16. CJR's own evaluators found it never closed a specific gap. Its Performance Year 5 report states plainly that the model did not reduce racial disparities in joint-replacement rates or quality, logged as an explicit lesson heading into TEAM's design.

17. The physician-owned hospital freeze that shapes today's market is 16 years old and still active. A 2010 ACA provision (Section 6001) banned new physician-owned hospitals from Medicare and froze roughly 250 existing ones at their 2010 bed and OR counts. Any physician-founded chain still operating today either got in before that door closed, or has been growing exclusively through non-hospital formats since.

18. CMMI's current director runs both TEAM and WISeR at once. Abe Sutton, the office's Deputy Administrator and Director, previously served on the National Economic Council and Domestic Policy Council in the first Trump administration.

19. CMS's own peer-reviewed research literature logged the "prior CJR exposure lowers your TEAM odds" mechanism independently. The 2025 PLOS ONE paper on CJR spillover effects, the same one that names LA, San Francisco, and Modesto as California's three mandatory CJR markets, was published before TEAM's participant list existed, meaning academic researchers flagged the exposure-history pattern as a variable worth studying ahead of, not in reaction to, the lottery outcome.

20. The whole 2009 pilot ran through a single administrative jurisdiction. ACE was restricted to physician-hospital organizations inside one Medicare Administrative Contractor region, Jurisdiction 4, covering only Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, and Colorado. The idea that's now nationwide in TEAM began confined to a single government back-office boundary, not a policy choice about which states mattered.
