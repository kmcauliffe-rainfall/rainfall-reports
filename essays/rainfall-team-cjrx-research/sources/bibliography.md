CMS primary sources — TEAM and CJR-X
- CMS TEAM model overview page, cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/team-model
- CMS TEAM Overview Webcast transcript, December 3, 2024. Presenters: Jen Lippy (Deputy Director, Division of Payment Models), Sacha Wolf (Technical Advisor, TEAM lead), Aaron Broun (Social Science Research Analyst). cms.gov/priorities/innovation/files/team-ovw-webinar-transcript.pdf — source for the Decarbonization and Resilience Initiative, the hurricane/disaster episode cancellation clause, the anti-kickback safe harbor, and the Area Deprivation Index pricing adjustment.
- CJR-X (Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement Expanded Model) — proposed April 10, 2026 (FY2027 IPPS proposed rule), finalized July 31, 2026, published in the Federal Register August 4, 2026. Effective date January 1, 2028 (pushed back from an originally proposed October 1, 2027).
- TEAM voluntary election window for former CJR/BPCI Advanced participants: January 1-31, 2025. Eligibility basis: participation in CJR through its last day, December 31, 2024, or in BPCI Advanced through December 31, 2025.
- 42 CFR 512.510(b)(1) — voluntary election eligibility criteria.

CMS primary sources — CJR (predecessor model)
- CMS. Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement (CJR) Model overview, MSA selection methodology, and annual reports. cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/cjr
- CMS / The Lewin Group. CJR Model: Performance Year 1 report — stratified random sampling methodology, 67 MSAs across eight strata, 104-MSA control group. cms.gov/priorities/innovation/Files/reports/cjr-firstannrptapp.pdf
- CJR Performance Year 5 evaluation report — the pandemic-era loss year, and the explicit finding that CJR did not reduce racial disparities in joint-replacement rates or quality.
- CJR Performance Year 6-7 reconciliation data — $78/episode average repayment (PY6) vs. $404/episode (PY7); $30.8 million cumulative savings through PY6 vs. $112.7 million across PY6-7 combined.
- 2018 CJR downsizing rule — cut mandatory participation from 67 to 34 MSAs, selected by highest historical episode spending (not a second random draw).
- Original CJR final rule — Federal Register public comments raising the statutory-authority objection (whether Section 1115A permits mandatory participation at all).

CMS primary sources — ACE Demonstration (2009 predecessor)
- Acute Care Episode (ACE) Demonstration — five participating hospitals: Baptist Health System (San Antonio, TX), Oklahoma Heart Hospital (Oklahoma City, OK), Exempla Saint Joseph Hospital (Denver, CO), Hillcrest Medical Center (Tulsa, OK), Lovelace Health System (Albuquerque, NM). Restricted to Medicare Administrative Contractor Jurisdiction 4 (TX, OK, NM, CO).

CMS primary sources — WISeR and CMMI strategy
- WISeR model — Wasteful and Inappropriate Service Reduction — launched January 1, 2026, six states (AZ, OH, OK, TX, NJ, WA), AI/ML-assisted prior authorization inside traditional Medicare fee-for-service. CMS public webinar with the American Medical Association citing $1.9-5.8 billion in targeted low-value spending.
- Sutton, Abe. "CMS Innovation Center Strategy to Make America Healthy Again." Published May 13, 2025. cms.gov/priorities/innovation/about/cms-innovation-center-strategy-make-america-healthy-again — full text rebuilt as a PDF in reference/CMS-Innovation-Center-Strategy-MAHA.pdf, since CMS never published a standalone PDF version.
- CBO analysis of CMMI's net fiscal impact — estimated $5.4 billion net increase in federal spending, 2011-2020.
- CMMI overall model track record — 70+ models tested since 2011 (as of January 2025); only 4 certified for nationwide expansion by the CMS Chief Actuary: Pioneer ACO, Medicare Diabetes Prevention Program, RSNAT (ambulance prior authorization), Home Health Value-Based Purchasing.

Statutory and legal
- Social Security Act, Section 1115A — statutory authority for CMS Innovation Center model testing; the legal basis requiring a control group, which produces the "lottery" selection mechanism.
- ACA Section 6001 — 2010 ban on new physician-owned hospitals entering Medicare, freezing roughly 250 existing ones at their 2010 bed/OR counts (grandfather clause).

Academic / peer-reviewed
- PLOS ONE (2025) — CJR spillover-effects paper naming Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Modesto as California's three mandatory CJR markets. Published before TEAM's participant list existed.
- Wenzhou total hip arthroplasty interrupted time-series study (PMC) — used as the citation for China's national DRG mandate, effective January 2021.

International systems
- NHS England. 2026/27 NHS Payment Scheme, Annex C: Guidance on Best Practice Tariffs — hip/knee replacement BPT criteria, national scope, no geographic sampling. england.nhs.uk
- National Joint Registry. NJR Best Practice Tariff compliance methodology, running since 2014. njrcentre.org.uk
- Busse, R., et al. "Germany: Understanding G-DRGs" — 2003 voluntary phase, 2004 nationwide mandate; adaptation of the Australian Refined DRG (AR-DRG v4.1) system as the base classification.
- Fetter, Robert; Thompson, John. Original DRG development at Yale University, 1970s, later adopted by Medicare for inpatient payment in 1983.
- France's T2A (tarification a l'activite) — phased rollout 2004-2008, applied uniformly to public and private hospitals.
- Japan's DPC/PDPS system — per-diem case-mix payment, running since 2003. All 82 university hospitals mandated in; community hospital adoption voluntary (approximately 1,786 of ~8,000 hospitals opted in as of 2024).
- China's National Healthcare Security Administration — nationwide DRG-based bundled payment mandate, effective January 2021.
