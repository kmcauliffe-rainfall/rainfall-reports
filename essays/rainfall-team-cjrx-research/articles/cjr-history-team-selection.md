# San Francisco Got Let Off the Hook Under CJR. Under TEAM, It Became California's Biggest Market.

*Los Angeles stayed a mandatory CJR market for nine straight years because it was expensive. San Francisco got downgraded to voluntary in 2018. Under TEAM's separate lottery, LA drew exactly one hospital — voluntarily. San Francisco drew 37, mandatorily, the most of any market in the state.*

Rainfall Health · September 2026

**In brief.** CMS ran two different lotteries, eight years apart, for two different bundled-payment models, using two different mechanisms — CJR's was a straight random draw, then a cost-based cut to 34 mandatory markets in 2018; TEAM's was its own separate stratified draw in 2024. There was no reason to expect one to predict the other. But zoom out to all 67 of CJR's original markets, and the ones CMS once forced into mandatory participation landed on TEAM's roster at nearly double the rate of the ones it let go voluntary — 35.3% versus 21.2%. California's three CJR markets are the extreme, ironic version of that pattern: Los Angeles, kept mandatory under CJR specifically for being expensive, has exactly one hospital on TEAM's list, and it's there by choice. San Francisco, downgraded to voluntary in 2018, became the single largest TEAM market in the state — 37 hospitals, all mandatory. Modesto, also downgraded in 2018, isn't on TEAM's list at all.

## The three California markets, before and after

California had three markets in CJR's original 67-MSA lottery: Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim, San Francisco-Oakland-Hayward (renamed San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont under the 2023 OMB delineation), and Modesto. All three were mandatory when CJR launched in 2016. In 2018, CMS cut mandatory participation to the 34 MSAs with the highest historical episode spending — a cost-based cut, not a second lottery. LA's spending was high enough to stay in. San Francisco's and Modesto's weren't; both dropped to voluntary.

Six years later, TEAM ran its own, unrelated stratified draw across the whole country. The outcome for these same three markets:

| Market | CJR status (2018+) | TEAM hospitals | TEAM participation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Los Angeles-Long Beach-Anaheim | Mandatory | 1 (Cedars-Sinai) | Voluntary |
| San Francisco-Oakland-Fremont | Voluntary | 37 | Mandatory |
| Modesto | Voluntary | 0 | Not selected |

LA is the market this folder's first article already covered in depth: the country's largest Medicare market, drawn out of TEAM's mandatory lottery, with its one hospital — Cedars-Sinai — there entirely by its own election. What hadn't been checked until now is what happened to the other two California markets that went through the same CJR history. The answer isn't "the same bad luck twice." San Francisco has more TEAM hospitals than any other market in California, mandatorily, and Modesto simply isn't on the list.

## Zooming out from three markets to sixty-seven

Three markets is an anecdote. The full CJR roster — all 67 MSAs from the original 2016 lottery — is a real sample. CMS no longer publishes the 2018 list of 34 mandatory MSAs as a single named table (its current CJR hospital list mixes mandatory and voluntary-continuing hospitals with no status column), so this analysis reconstructs it: re-applying CMS's own stated 2018 criterion — highest average wage-adjusted historical episode payment — to the original 67-MSA roster, using CMS's own 2015-rule payment data. The reconstruction reproduces all three known outcomes above as a check on itself before being used for anything else.

Against TEAM's 189 selected CBSAs nationally (out of 387 total metropolitan CBSAs under the current OMB delineation — a 48.8% baseline rate if the draw were blind to CJR history):

| Group | Selected into TEAM | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| All 67 original CJR markets | 19 of 67 | 28.4% |
| 34 former-mandatory CJR markets | 12 of 34 | 35.3% |
| 33 former-voluntary-tier CJR markets | 7 of 33 | 21.2% |
| National baseline (all metro CBSAs) | 189 of 387 | 48.8% |

Two things are true at once, and they point in different directions. First: any market that went through CJR at all — mandatory or voluntary tier — had roughly half the national-average odds of landing on TEAM's roster. Second: within that group, the markets CMS once forced into mandatory participation did *better*, not worse, than the ones it let go voluntary. San Francisco isn't a fluke inside the data; the pattern it fits is real, just running the opposite direction from what "the market that lost twice" implies.

## What this doesn't establish

TEAM's selection, like CJR's original lottery, is a stratified random draw — a 189-of-387 result could reflect a real relationship between market characteristics and TEAM's stratification variables (population, historical spending, something correlated with having been a large CJR market in the first place), or it could be within the ordinary noise of a draw this size. No significance test has been run on this, and none is claimed here. What can be said cleanly: in this one draw, the historical direction of "was CMS willing to force this market to participate before" ran toward *more* TEAM exposure, not less — the opposite of what a simple "same unlucky markets" story would predict, and the exact opposite of what happened to LA specifically inside its own market's hospital count.

The 34-mandatory-MSA list itself is a reconstruction, not a CMS-published table copied verbatim — see "How this was verified" below before treating any single MSA's status as certain.

## What's still unverified

Whether TEAM's own stratification variables (the ones CMS used to draw its 189 CBSAs) include anything that correlates mechanically with CJR history — population size and historical joint-replacement spending are plausible candidates, since both featured in CJR's own stratification — hasn't been checked. If TEAM's strata happen to overlap with CJR's, the pattern here could be a byproduct of both models drawing on similar underlying market characteristics rather than any information flow between the two programs. This would need TEAM's own published stratification methodology cross-referenced against CJR's, which hasn't been done here.
