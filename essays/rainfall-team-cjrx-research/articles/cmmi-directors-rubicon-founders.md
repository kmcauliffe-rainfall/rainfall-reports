Three Directors, One Firm: Who Actually Runs the Innovation Center

The last three people to run CMS's Innovation Center under Republican administrations all built their careers at the same kind of company, and two of them at the same firm. Here is the documented lineage, and the one document that would tell us whether it matters financially.

Rainfall Health, September 2026

In brief. Adam Boehler ran CMMI first, from 2018 to 2020, coming from Landmark Health, a value based home care company he founded. Brad Smith succeeded him, from 2020 to 2021, coming from Aspire Health, a value based palliative care company he founded. Abe Sutton runs it now, from 2025 to the present, coming from Rubicon Founders, the investment firm Boehler started right after leaving CMMI, where Sutton co founded two more value based risk companies, Honest Health and Evergreen Nephrology. None of the three came from a hospital, a health system, or an insurer. All three came from companies built specifically to help providers take on and manage financial risk under Medicare. This pattern is already noted in industry trade press at the general level. What hasn't been said plainly is how literal the connection is between two of these three. Sutton didn't just work in a similar kind of company. He worked at the firm founded by his own predecessor's predecessor.

The three directors, in order. Adam Boehler founded Landmark Health in 2014, an in home medical care company built around taking on financial risk for the sickest, highest cost Medicare patients. He was appointed CMMI Director in April 2018, during the first Trump administration, and also held the title Deputy Administrator of CMS and Senior Advisor for Value Based Transformation to HHS Secretary Alex Azar. He left CMS in 2019 to become the first CEO of the U.S. International Development Finance Corporation. In April 2021 he founded Rubicon Founders, a Nashville based health care investment firm explicitly focused on building transformational value based care companies, backed by Oak HC/FT and Welsh, Carson, Anderson and Stowe.

Brad Smith succeeded Boehler as CMMI Director in 2020, serving through the end of the first Trump administration. Before that, he was CEO of Aspire Health, a company he co founded that provided home based palliative care under value based, at risk payment arrangements. After leaving CMMI, he founded Russell Street Ventures.

Abe Sutton is the current director, appointed in January 2025 under the second Trump administration. Before that, from 2021 onward, he was a Principal at Rubicon Founders, Boehler's firm, where he co founded Honest Health, a primary care physician enablement company, and Evergreen Nephrology, a nephrology enablement company, both structured around helping physicians take on financial risk under value based payment. Earlier, from 2017 to 2019, he'd worked at the National Economic Council, the Domestic Policy Council, and HHS as an advisor to Secretary Azar, the same administration official Boehler was simultaneously advising.

All three men, and their two predecessor successor companies, were confirmed on a shared stage together. Fierce Healthcare reported that Boehler, Smith, and then Director Liz Fowler appeared on the same CMMI history panel at the ViVE 2023 conference.

What actually connects them. The general pattern, CMMI leadership under Republican administrations drawing from value based care company founders rather than from hospitals or payers, has already been noted at the macro level. The Keckley Report, a health policy newsletter, wrote in May 2025 that Sutton brings prior experience in federal health agencies and operating insight from private equity backed ventures, grouping him explicitly with Boehler and Smith as a recognized type.

What that coverage doesn't spell out is how direct the link actually is between two of the three. Sutton didn't independently arrive at a similar career path. He worked, specifically, at the firm his own predecessor's predecessor founded, immediately after that predecessor left the same government office Sutton now runs. This isn't the same industry. It's the same firm, one hop removed.

What kind of company, specifically, and what it isn't. It matters exactly what these companies do, because the category has more than one variety and they're not interchangeable. Landmark Health, Aspire Health, Honest Health, and Evergreen Nephrology are all provider risk enablement companies. They help physicians and physician groups take on financial accountability for a whole population of patients, in primary care, home based care, and kidney care, under models like ACOs and global capitation.

That is a different category from the companies TEAM specifically pushes hospitals toward, like naviHealth or Signify Health, which manage discrete surgical episodes and post acute care for a single bundled procedure. Nobody in this lineage, Boehler, Smith, or Sutton, built a company in that narrower, episode or post acute category. So the accurate claim is that CMMI's recent leadership has consistently come from the value based risk vendor world in general, not from the specific orthopedic bundle or post acute vendor niche that TEAM hospitals actually have to engage with. Stating it as the exact machinery TEAM forces hospitals to buy would overreach past what's true.

What isn't established here. None of this shows that Sutton, Boehler, or Smith personally profited, or profits now, from any CMMI model, including TEAM or CJR X. Federal appointees at Sutton's level are required to file a public financial disclosure report, an OGE Form 278, at the start of their appointment, listing assets, and are generally required to divest or recuse from matters affecting entities in which they hold a financial interest. Whether Sutton retained any equity or profit interest in Rubicon Founders, Honest Health, or Evergreen Nephrology after joining CMS in January 2025, and whether any ethics agreement covers it, is exactly what that disclosure form would show. We don't have that document. Locating it, through OGE, HHS's ethics office, or a FOIA request, is the one step that would move this from a documented career pattern to a documented financial interest, and it's an open, checkable next step rather than an assumption either way.
