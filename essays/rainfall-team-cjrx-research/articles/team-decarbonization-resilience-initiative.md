# The Climate Program Hiding Inside a Medicare Payment Model

*TEAM is a mandatory Medicare payment model for surgery. It also contains a voluntary climate-reporting program that has nothing to do with payment at all — and CMS's own staff couldn't agree on its name.*

Rainfall Health · September 2026

**In brief.** Buried inside TEAM — the mandatory, five-year, financially binding surgical payment model discussed everywhere else on this site — is a small, entirely voluntary program called the Decarbonization and Resilience Initiative. It has two parts: technical assistance for any hospital that wants it, and an option to report Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions using the standard corporate carbon-accounting framework. None of it touches payment. A hospital's carbon footprint changes nothing about its target price, its quality score, or its reconciliation. CMS calls it the first initiative of its kind in an Innovation Center model — and its own presenters gave it two different names in the same public webcast.

## What it actually is

The source for this is CMS's own TEAM Overview Webcast, held December 3, 2024, ahead of the model's January 2026 launch. Toward the end of a 45-minute presentation covering participation tracks, quality measures, target pricing, and payment waivers, CMS staff introduced a section on what they called, at that point, the "voluntary Decarbonization and Resilience Initiative."

It has exactly two components. First, technical assistance: individualized benchmark feedback reports, public recognition, and help identifying tools to measure emissions, available to any interested TEAM participant. Second, a voluntary reporting option to capture Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions, defined under the Greenhouse Gas Protocol — the same corporate carbon-accounting standard used well outside health care. Scope 1 covers a hospital's direct emissions: its own boilers, generators, fleet vehicles. Scope 2 covers emissions from the electricity and energy it purchases.

## What it isn't

Nothing here is tied to money. TEAM's actual financial mechanics — target prices, the Composite Quality Score, stop-gain and stop-loss limits, reconciliation — are unaffected by anything a hospital reports or doesn't report through this initiative. A hospital that fully participates in the emissions-reporting option and a hospital that ignores the whole thing entirely face identical financial exposure under TEAM. This is disclosure and technical assistance, not a lever.

## Why CMS says it's there at all

CMS's stated rationale, from the same webcast: the initiative is meant to help hospitals address "threats to the nation's health and its healthcare system presented by climate change and the effects of hospital carbon emissions on health outcomes." The framing is explicitly public-health, not environmental policy on its own terms — the argument connects climate effects (heat, disaster-driven disruption to care delivery) and hospital emissions back to health outcomes CMS already has a mandate to care about, rather than treating emissions reduction as a goal in itself.

## CMS couldn't settle on its own name for it

One small, verifiable detail: the initiative gets two different names within the same recorded call. Early in the transcript, when the agenda is introduced, it's called the "**Decarbonization and Resilience Initiative**." Later, when the actual presenter hands off to cover the section, it's introduced as the "**Decarbonization and Climate Resilience Initiative**" — same speaker, same webcast, two names, eleven minutes apart. Neither is obviously the "wrong" one; CMS's own materials appear not to have settled the question before going live with the recording.

## What's still open

Whether any California TEAM hospitals opted into the emissions-reporting piece, and whether CMS has published anything from the benchmark reports since the program started in January 2026, isn't established here. Because it's a separate voluntary track from the roster of mandatory and voluntary *payment* participants, a hospital's presence on the TEAM participant list says nothing about whether it opted into decarbonization reporting too — that would need CMS's TEAM webpage directly, or a records request, since it isn't part of the participant roster used elsewhere in this analysis.
