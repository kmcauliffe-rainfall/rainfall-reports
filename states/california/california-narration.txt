CMS TEAM in California: Reading the State's Roster, Risk and Readiness

By Jennifer Tim-Diamond for Rainfall Health. Published September 2026.

This is a briefing for hospital executives on what the California TEAM roster and current CMS performance data show, and what changes in Performance Year 2. There are 107 participating hospitals across 11 CBSAs, and Performance Year 2 begins in January 2027.

Section one. At a glance.

California carries the largest state roster in the Transforming Episode Accountability Model: 107 hospitals across 11 CBSAs, or about 15 percent of the 720 hospital national list. Of those, 106 participate on a mandatory basis and 1 by voluntary election. All are committed for the model's full five performance years, running from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2030.

Two findings shape how California executives should prioritize readiness work.

The first is that the state's exposure is concentrated, not distributed. Two markets hold 67 of the 107 hospitals. San Francisco, Oakland, Fremont, with 37 hospitals, is the second largest TEAM market in the country, and Riverside, San Bernardino, Ontario, with 30 hospitals, is the fourth largest. That puts two of the national top five markets inside one state.

The second is that readmission performance runs in opposite directions across the cohorts that matter. On the two conditions that map directly to TEAM surgical episode categories, California TEAM hospitals average better than the national figure. Hip and knee replacement comes in at zero point nine eight zero against one point zero zero four nationally, and CABG at zero point nine nine three against one point zero zero two. On medical conditions the position reverses, with pneumonia at one point zero one four against one point zero zero one. Section three explains why that split matters more in Performance Year 2 than it did in Year 1.

Here is a quick summary of the state's position. There are 107 participating hospitals, 106 mandatory and 1 voluntary, representing the largest state roster and about 15 percent of the national list. There are 11 CBSAs, which brings multi market operating and contracting complexity. The largest market is San Francisco, Oakland, Fremont, with 37 sites, the second largest TEAM market nationally. The performance period runs from January 1, 2026 through December 31, 2030, five years of episode accountability already under way. The Medicare population in scope is roughly three and a half million Original Medicare beneficiaries out of about seven point two million total, since TEAM episodes apply to Original Medicare only. And the surgical readmission signal, hip and knee ERR at zero point nine eight zero versus one point zero zero four nationally, is favorable on the largest TEAM surgical cohort.

A note on ownership concentration. Roughly a third of the California roster, 32 of 107 hospitals, maps to a recognizable multi hospital system, and the single largest system accounts for 17 sites, just under 16 percent of the state list. Where a system holds a double digit share of a state roster, episode standards, post acute contracting, and data infrastructure tend to be set centrally, which changes who needs to be at the table for readiness decisions.

Section three. Readmission performance where TEAM pays.

TEAM holds a hospital accountable for spending and quality across the 30 days following an episode triggering procedure, so pre-existing readmission performance is the closest available read on how a hospital will fare. The CMS Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program public use file reports an Excess Readmission Ratio for each hospital and condition. This ratio compares predicted to expected 30 day readmissions given the hospital's case mix. A value above one means more readmissions than expected. A value below one means fewer.

The analysis in this report joins the California TEAM roster to that file using each hospital's CMS Certification Number, so every participating hospital is matched exactly. Of the 107 hospitals, 96 have at least one reportable ratio in the current file, and 11 fall below CMS reporting thresholds across all six conditions. The measurement window is July 2021 through June 2024.

Figure three shows the mean Excess Readmission Ratio by condition, comparing California TEAM hospitals to the national average. The pattern is consistent and worth stating plainly. California TEAM hospitals sit below the national mean on both surgical cohorts that map to TEAM episode categories. Hip and knee replacement comes in at zero point nine eight zero versus one point zero zero four, and CABG at zero point nine nine three versus one point zero zero two. They sit above the national mean on every medical condition, most visibly pneumonia, at one point zero one four versus one point zero zero one, heart attack at one point zero zero eight, and heart failure at one point zero zero six.

The operational reading is that the state's surgical pathways, which are what TEAM pays on, are performing comparatively well, while the transition of care for medical admissions is where readmissions exceed expectation. Those two things usually draw on the same infrastructure, discharge planning, post acute placement, and follow up contact, so the gap points to where capability exists and where it has not yet been extended.

Figure four shows the share of hospitals above the expectation line for each condition. Averages conceal spread, and here the spread carries the more useful message. California's favorable hip and knee average is not broad based. 49 percent of reporting California TEAM hospitals still sit above one point zero zero on that measure, against 48 percent nationally. The state average is pulled down by strong performers rather than lifted by uniform performance. On pneumonia the prevalence gap is wider and in the other direction, at 57 percent against 47 percent nationally.

For an individual hospital, the implication is that the state level picture is not a proxy for its own position. The actionable unit is the hospital's own Excess Readmission Ratio by condition, which is published and can be pulled directly from the same file.

Here are the full condition level figures. For hip and knee replacement, a TEAM surgical cohort, the California mean ratio is zero point nine eight zero against a national mean of one point zero zero four, with 49 percent of California hospitals above one point zero zero compared with 48 percent nationally. For CABG, also a TEAM surgical cohort, the California mean is zero point nine nine three against a national mean of one point zero zero two, with 42 percent of California hospitals above the line compared with 50 percent nationally. For heart attack, a medical condition, the California mean is one point zero zero eight against a national mean of one point zero zero two, with 57 percent of California hospitals above the line compared with 50 percent nationally. For heart failure, a medical condition, the California mean is one point zero zero six against a national mean of one point zero zero one, with 54 percent above the line compared with 49 percent nationally. For pneumonia, a medical condition, the California mean is one point zero one four against a national mean of one point zero zero one, with 57 percent above the line compared with 47 percent nationally. For COPD, a medical condition, the California mean is one point zero zero two against a national mean of one point zero zero one, with 49 percent above the line compared with 47 percent nationally.

One important clarification. The Excess Readmission Ratio is a performance ratio, not a payment outcome. A hospital's actual HRRP payment adjustment is calculated by CMS from inputs that are not published in this file, so none of these figures should be read as a penalty rate or a dollar amount.

Section four. What changes in Performance Year 2.

Performance Year 2 runs through calendar 2027. Four changes are already final and warrant planning attention now.

First, the quality measure set turns over. CMS PSI 90 applies in Performance Year 1 only. From Performance Year 2, the composite quality score is built from the hybrid hospital wide all cause readmission measure, Hospital Harm, Falls with Injury, Hospital Harm, Postoperative Respiratory Failure, and the 30 day risk standardized death rate among surgical inpatients with complications. The hip and knee patient reported outcome measure continues for lower extremity joint replacement episodes, and an information transfer measure joins in Performance Year 3 for joint replacement and spinal fusion episodes. Two consequences follow. Readiness narratives built around PSI 90 need retiring, and the hospital wide readmission measure, which spans medical as well as surgical admissions, carries more of the score. Read against section three, that is precisely where California's roster is weakest.

Second, spinal fusion episode definitions expand. The fiscal year 2027 Inpatient Prospective Payment System final rule established new diagnosis related groups, numbers 523 through 525, for extensive and complex spinal fusion, effective for discharges on or after October 1, 2026, and brought them into TEAM's existing spinal fusion episode category. Coding, documentation, and service line forecasting should be updated before the October effective date rather than after the first reconciliation.

Third, target price mechanics tighten. Beginning in Performance Year 2, CMS applies prospective normalization factors calculated across the full three year baseline period rather than the most recent year alone, alongside updated risk adjustment coefficients. Baseline periods for the new quality measures are calendar 2025 and 2026, meaning performance already recorded is being measured.

Fourth, the health equity reporting pathway is closed, not optional. The fiscal year 2026 Inpatient Prospective Payment System final rule removed the voluntary health equity plan, the health related social needs data reporting element, and the decarbonization and resilience initiative from TEAM. Organizations still carrying these as pending deliverables can retire that work. The same rule expanded the skilled nursing facility three day rule waiver and adopted version 28 of the hierarchical condition category model in the payment methodology, both of which bear directly on post acute routing and risk capture.

One more development worth watching, looking past TEAM, is CJR-X. The fiscal year 2027 rule also finalized a nationwide mandatory joint replacement model, called CJR-X, effective January 1, 2028 for hospitals paid under the Inpatient Prospective Payment System, explicitly excluding hospitals that participate in TEAM. For California's 107 TEAM hospitals, the practical effect is that they remain inside TEAM's 30 day construct through 2030, while non participating peers move to a 90 day joint replacement episode that also covers outpatient procedures and adds ankle replacement. Joint replacement strategy built for a 30 day window will not transfer directly to peers, partners, or acquisition targets operating on a 90 day one.

Section five. Executive priorities.

First, confirm roster position by Certification Number, not by name. Hospital names differ between internal systems and CMS files; certification numbers do not. The current participant list is the authoritative record of who is in, on what basis, and for how long.

Second, pull your own condition level Excess Readmission Ratio. The state picture is mixed enough that it says little about any single hospital. The published file gives each hospital its own position on all six conditions and is the fastest available read on 30 day performance.

Third, extend surgical discharge practice to medical admissions. California's roster performs well on surgical readmissions and less well on medical ones. With the hospital wide readmission measure carrying more weight from Performance Year 2, this gap moves from a quality concern to a scored one.

Fourth, re-plan post acute routing against the evidence. Evaluations of earlier bundled payment programs found that savings came primarily from reduced institutional post acute use rather than from inpatient care, and without measurable harm to readmissions, emergency visits, or mortality. The expanded skilled nursing facility three day waiver widens the options available to do this deliberately.

Fifth, set expectations from the evidence base, not from the model's ambition. In the first year of the mandatory joint replacement program, institutional post acute discharge fell by two point nine percentage points, while total episode spending did not change significantly. A separate voluntary program, evaluated over a longer window, did show episode payments falling by roughly one thousand one hundred sixty six dollars more than comparison hospitals. Savings are achievable, but they accrue through sustained post acute change, not in the first reconciliation.

Section six. Methods, sources, and limitations.

Here is how each element of this analysis was built. Roster counts and the CBSA breakdown come from the CMS TEAM Participant List, the 2026 second quarter release as of April 15, 2026, parsed and vendored into the underlying repository, and are computed at render time. The readmission analysis comes from the CMS Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program public use file, dataset 9 n 3 s dash k d b 3, covering the window from July 2021 through June 2024, and is also computed at render time. The roster to HRRP linkage uses the CMS Certification Number for an exact match, and 96 of the 107 hospitals carry reportable data. Policy and rule changes are drawn from Federal Register final rules and title 42 of the Code of Federal Regulations, section 512.547, and are cited rather than computed. Bundled payment evidence comes from peer reviewed evaluations of the Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement model and the Bundled Payments for Care Improvement initiative, and is also cited. Episode cost exposure is not published at the hospital level and is therefore not presented in this report.

On roster provenance: counts are parsed from the CMS TEAM Participant List, 2026 second quarter release as of April 15, 2026, and yield a California total of 107 hospitals. San Francisco, Oakland, Fremont has 37; Riverside, San Bernardino, Ontario has 30; and Bay Area plus Inland Empire together account for 73, or 68 percent. These reconcile to the editorial anchors held for this report. CMS republishes the list quarterly, so counts should always be read together with their list vintage.

On why no dollar exposure figures appear: hospital level TEAM target prices, baseline episode spending, and episode volumes are not published by CMS. Estimating exposure in dollars would require a hospital's own claims history and reconciliation inputs. Producing such figures from public data alone would give a false impression of precision, so this report presents only what the public files support.

On limitations: the HRRP measurement window, July 2021 through June 2024, predates TEAM's first performance year, so it describes the position hospitals entered the model with rather than performance under it. The Excess Readmission Ratio reflects risk adjusted readmissions only and is not a measure of episode cost. Hospitals below CMS case thresholds have no published ratio, which is why 11 of the 107 California hospitals do not appear in the condition level analysis. Counts reflect the participant list identified above and will change with each quarterly CMS update.

Frequently asked questions.

Why do some documents show a different California hospital count? The participant list is republished quarterly, and counts move as CMS updates it. The figure used here, 107, is taken from the 2026 second quarter release as of April 15, 2026, and reconciles exactly to that file. Any count should be read together with the list vintage it came from.

Is the Los Angeles market in TEAM? Only to the extent CMS selected it. The Los Angeles, Long Beach, Anaheim CBSA contributes one hospital to the participant list; the metropolitan hospital market as a whole is not in the model.

Does a favorable state average mean a given hospital is well positioned? No. As the condition level data show, roughly half of reporting California TEAM hospitals sit above the expectation line even on the cohort where the state average is favorable. Position should be read at the hospital level.

Where should finance and operations start? With the hospital's own condition level Excess Readmission Ratio and its post acute referral pattern. Both are available now, and both are where the published evidence locates most of the achievable change.
